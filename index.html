How Our Alert System Works

Our disaster alert system continuously monitors environmental conditions through a network of monitoring points across the region. Each monitoring point tracks:

  • Rainfall Index: Measures precipitation intensity in mm/h
  • Wind Speed: Real-time wind velocity in km/h
  • Altitude: Elevation data for flood risk assessment
  • Seismic Activity: Ground vibration measurements
  • Cloud Index: Cloud cover percentage indicating storm potential

Alert Priority Levels

🔴 High Priority

Immediate action required. Indicates severe weather conditions, imminent flooding, or dangerous seismic activity.

🟡 Medium Priority

Important notice requiring attention. Possible weather deterioration or rising risk levels.

🟢 Low Priority

General information and updates about environmental conditions.

Risk Assessment

The system calculates risk levels based on multiple factors:

  • Danger Level < 30%: Normal conditions, regular monitoring
  • Danger Level 30-70%: Elevated risk, increased surveillance
  • Danger Level > 70%: Critical situation, emergency protocols activated

Emergency Message System

Authorized personnel can broadcast emergency messages through our alert system. Messages are:

  • Instantly distributed to all connected monitoring points
  • Categorized by priority level for quick assessment
  • Archived for future reference and pattern analysis
  • Automatically triggered when danger levels exceed thresholds

Disaster Mitigation Scenarios & Alert Responses

1. High Rainfall Index

Scenario: Continuous or sudden increase in rainfall intensity and duration.
Possible Disaster: Flooding, landslides (especially in high-altitude or deforested regions).

  • Low Alert:
    • Notify local communities of potential heavy rainfall.
    • Check functionality of drainage systems.
    • Update weather forecasts every 3–6 hours.
  • Medium Alert:
    • Prepare temporary shelters and emergency supplies.
    • Close low-lying roadways and bridges as precaution.
    • Begin controlled release of water from reservoirs.
  • High Alert:
    • Immediate evacuation from flood-prone zones.
    • Deploy emergency response teams.
    • Shut down vulnerable power and communication lines.
    • Activate real-time alert systems (SMS, radio, satellite beacons).

2. High Wind Speed + High Cloud Index

Scenario: Rapidly increasing wind speed with thick cloud coverage (storm formation).
Possible Disaster: Cyclones, hurricanes, or severe thunderstorms.

  • Low Alert:
    • Inform public of possible storm formation.
    • Check emergency shelters and communication lines.
    • Keep local fishing and aviation activities on standby.
  • Medium Alert:
    • Close ports and coastal activities.
    • Secure power lines, roofs, and loose structures.
    • Ensure emergency power and food storage at critical facilities.
  • High Alert:
    • Enforce full evacuation of coastal and low-lying areas.
    • Activate continuous weather radar and satellite monitoring.
    • Deploy rapid response units and emergency communication nodes.

3. High Altitude + Heavy Rainfall

Scenario: Steep terrain or mountain regions experiencing heavy or sustained rainfall.
Possible Disaster: Landslides, flash floods.

  • Low Alert:
    • Inspect slope stability and drainage channels.
    • Educate locals on identifying landslide warning signs.
  • Medium Alert:
    • Restrict movement in vulnerable slope zones.
    • Prepare evacuation plans and transport routes.
    • Position rescue teams at critical access points.
  • High Alert:
    • Immediate evacuation from high-risk slopes.
    • Shut off mountain roads and tunnels.
    • Deploy drones or satellite surveillance for real-time landslide detection.

4. Flood-Prone Region + High Rainfall Index + High Cloud Index

Scenario: Combination of dense clouds, heavy rainfall, and geographical susceptibility.
Possible Disaster: Flash floods or major river flooding.

  • Low Alert:
    • Notify regional flood management teams.
    • Conduct inspection of dams and levees.
  • Medium Alert:
    • Pre-position rescue boats and relief materials.
    • Conduct public announcements and evacuation readiness drills.
  • High Alert:
    • Mandatory evacuation of affected flood zones.
    • Close transport networks through waterlogged zones.
    • Prioritize communication through mesh or satellite networks for isolated areas.

5. Increased Seismic Activity + High Altitude

Scenario: Earth tremors or fault line shifts in mountain or hilly regions.
Possible Disaster: Earthquake-triggered landslides, aftershocks.

  • Low Alert:
    • Monitor ground vibration levels continuously.
    • Educate communities about earthquake safety measures.
  • Medium Alert:
    • Suspend high-altitude construction and mining operations.
    • Prepare medical and fire response teams in nearby towns.
  • High Alert:
    • Enforce immediate evacuation from unstable slopes and infrastructure.
    • Isolate gas and electric lines to prevent secondary hazards.
    • Deploy search and rescue drones post-event.

6. High Cloud Index + Seismic Activity + Rainfall

Scenario: Cloud formation and rainfall after minor seismic activity (can lead to mudslides or soil liquefaction).
Possible Disaster: Combined flood–landslide risk.

  • Low Alert:
    • Increase frequency of geological monitoring.
    • Begin terrain scanning using satellite data.
  • Medium Alert:
    • Restrict road access through hilly passes.
    • Warn local populations of potential unstable ground.
  • High Alert:
    • Full evacuation of risk zones.
    • Ground-based and aerial monitoring for early detection of mudslides.
    • Coordinate with relief agencies for immediate intervention.

7. Cloud Index + Wind Speed (Without Rainfall)

Scenario: Formation of dry storms or dust storms (common in arid zones).
Possible Disaster: Dust storms or sandstorms.

  • Low Alert:
    • Issue advisory for visibility and respiratory risks.
    • Encourage indoor safety during high winds.
  • Medium Alert:
    • Suspend outdoor transportation and aviation temporarily.
    • Set up emergency medical units for respiratory issues.
  • High Alert:
    • Declare no-travel zones.
    • Activate air filtration in essential facilities (hospitals, schools).
    • Emergency broadcasts through resilient communication nodes.

About Our Project

We're building a web-based simulation platform that showcases how resilient communication systems can operate even in the most challenging and hazardous environments. By integrating mesh networks, satellite links, and delay-tolerant protocols, our project demonstrates how reliable disaster alerts and messaging capabilities can be maintained in remote or regions with limited connectivity.

Our goal is simple yet vital — We strive to support communities in remote areas during natural calamities by exploring technologies that ensure communication remains possible when it matters most.

Key Features

  • Advanced Environmental Monitoring: Real-time tracking of rainfall, wind speed, cloud coverage, and more
  • Terrain Analysis: Integration of altitude data and flood-prone region mapping
  • Seismic Activity Monitoring: Tracking of geological instabilities and earthquake risks
  • Predictive Alerts: 24-hour advance warning system based on multiple environmental factors
  • Risk-Based Assessment: Customized alert levels based on regional characteristics

Understanding Disaster Risk & Response in India

Climate Overview

India: Tropical monsoon climate with four seasons — winter, summer, monsoon, and post-monsoon. Climate varies by region: arid in the west, humid in the east, extreme in the north, and moderate in the south.

Chennai: Tropical wet and dry climate. Hot and humid year-round, with rainfall mainly from the northeast monsoon (Oct–Dec). Vulnerable to cyclones and urban flooding.

How Weather Escalates into Disasters

  • North: Cloudbursts and glacier melts cause flash floods and landslides.
  • East & Northeast: Monsoon rains flood rivers like the Brahmaputra and Ganga.
  • West: Droughts and heatwaves in arid zones like Rajasthan.
  • South & Coastal: Cyclones and storm surges affect Tamil Nadu, Odisha, Andhra Pradesh.
  • Central: Irregular rainfall leads to both floods and droughts.

Government Initiatives

  • NDMA: National Disaster Management Authority — policy and planning.
  • NDRF: Specialized rescue and relief force.
  • IMD: Weather forecasts via Mausam and Damini.
  • SACHET & CAP: Early warning systems for mobile/web alerts.
  • ISRO: Satellites like INSAT and RISAT for real-time disaster monitoring.

Your Role in Safety

  • Stay updated with alerts from IMD and NDMA.
  • Prepare emergency kits with essentials.
  • Know your region’s risks and evacuation routes.
  • Trust only verified sources — avoid rumors.
  • Use mesh-network tools (like our project) to stay connected in remote areas.

How It Works

Our system continuously monitors multiple environmental factors to predict and assess potential disasters up to 24 hours in advance. By analyzing the correlation between wind speed, rainfall index, cloud coverage, and geographical features, we can:

  • Predict severe weather events like cyclones and storms
  • Assess flooding risks in vulnerable areas
  • Evaluate landslide risks in high-altitude regions
  • Provide timely evacuation recommendations
